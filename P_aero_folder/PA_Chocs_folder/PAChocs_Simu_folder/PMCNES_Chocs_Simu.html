Méthodes de modélisation pour l'analyse en chocs

Numerical methods are emerging as the future standard for predicting shock levels in spacecraft structures. However, their efficacy hinges on the availability of robust and precise modeling strategies, coupled with simulation techniques that balance computational efficiency with physical fidelity. The primary objectives, as revealed by the challenges of shock level prediction, encompass to:

  • Understand and analyze the shock source, including assessing its representativeness, particularly when based on specifications.
  • Control the definition and application of the shock source, identify appropriate modeling techniques, and evaluate the representativeness, necessity, and potential outcomes of each approach.
  • Model the propagation and transmission of shocks through structures and across interfaces.
  • Define post‑processing strategies and sizing criteria for structural design.

In this context, the following articles or communications focus on comparing numerical methodologies for shock level prediction and creating a set of engineering recommendations that advocate the adoption of numerical tools in shock analysis, based on a parametric study of the impact of a stainless‑steel ball on an aluminum plate, with and without the plate being equipped. Rather than focusing solely on case‑specific model correlations with experimental data, the objective is instead to establish a comprehensive framework of modeling techniques that prioritize accuracy, robustness, and simplicity while ensuring applicability to a wide range of structures, from simplified assemblies to complex spacecraft systems.
